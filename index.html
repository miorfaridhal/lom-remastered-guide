Quests by artifact

All 68 events. Each sits under the artifact whose land it starts in. Switch to play order for a safe sequence that respects prerequisites and the quests that can be missed. Tick a quest when you finish it; the status tags update. Tap a quest to see where it starts, what it needs and a step-by-step route.

After every event: go home, talk to Li'l Cactus, go downstairs to watch him write it on the leaf, then go back upstairs and save. Routes are put together from Legend of Mana Info, StrategyWiki and the Steam Complete Unlock Guide. A line marked Path is a move-by-move direction string from Legend of Mana Info. A few of its pages say they need an overhaul, so if a path does not match the screen, go by the steps above it.

Abilities and how to unlock them

You start with eight abilities. The other 18 unlock by winning battles with the listed abilities equipped. Tick what you have; each card says whether it is ready to train or what is still missing.

No battle counts here. The game shows no progress bar, and the only numbers published are from the Japanese version, so they could mislead on Android. Win battles with the listed abilities equipped. You can only carry two abilities at a time, and each one keeps its count when you swap it out, so train one pair, then swap. The Android remaster renamed two abilities: Retreat is now Backstep and Bash is now Catapult. The cards show both names.

Special techniques by weapon

Every weapon type has its own list. Each technique needs a set of abilities. A technique shows Ready once you have every ability it needs, and Locked with the abilities to unlock first when you do not.

How learning works. A technique needs battles won with its weapon equipped, plus battles won with each ability it lists. The game hides both counts. Ability counts add up across every weapon and never reset when you swap an ability out. Some techniques list three or four abilities, but you only have two ability slots, and you do not need them all equipped at once: train one pair, then swap to the next pair. Your starting weapon also grants one free technique. Names and requirements follow the remaster; where a name changed, the old one shows beside it. Master Moves are a separate thing: the thrust, jump and uppercut heavy attacks listed on your weapon, which tempering cards can replace (see the Forge tab). Treat Ready as "prerequisites met" and tick Learned when the game confirms it.

      Drop finder

      Search any item or monster, or browse the best targets. Each source shows the drop level, the monster level it needs and about how many kills one drop takes. Tick Need to add an item to your farm list.

      How drops work. Each kill rolls one of eight drop levels. Levels 1 to 4 can happen at any monster level; level 5 needs monster Lv 16+, level 6 needs 32+, level 7 needs 48+ and level 8 needs 64+. The odds halve at each step: 50%, 25%, 12.5%, 6.25% and so on. Monster level is the land's level minus 3, and a land's level is its distance from Home plus its place in your placement order. By that formula a normal-mode land tops out in the 30s or low 40s, so the rarest drops mostly need a Polter Box pet (sync: Rare Item), a lucky drop (your Luck out of 255), or New Game Plus. That is a fan analysis, not a measured result.

      Pet ranking

      All 64 pets from highest to lowest base stats, with where to get each one. Tick the ones you own.

      What the numbers are. Each pet has eight base ratings from 2 to 10. They show how well it can grow, not its final stats; levels and food decide those. Demi-humans (Chobin Hood to Tomato Man) are not in eggs: they follow you from a land once its element is high enough. An egg hatches a random species from its family, and lands close to Home lean toward the weaker ones.

      Best picks

      Catching and feeding

      • Catching an egg. You can only take it asleep. Wait out of its sight until it dozes off, or drop up to 4 fruit or meat in its path; one or two usually do it. Food it dislikes keeps it awake. The corral holds five.
      • What hatches. When the corral is full, the sell prompt lists every egg with its HP. HP differs by species, which lets you tell eggs apart. Reported for poltergeist eggs: Chess Knight 21, Cursed Doll 25, Dainslaif 32, Polter Box 68.
      • How often to feed. A pet eats up to 3 fruit or meat each time it gains a level. Reaching level 99 takes about 98 feedings, up to 294 items. Stats and level cap at 99, and each stat grows by at most 3.75 a level.
      • Foods that matter. Dialaurel (1,000 Lucre after The Field Trip, #63) raises Power and Magic Defense growth. Cabbadillo raises Defense and the physical defenses but lowers Charm and Magic Defense. Rocket Papaya gives +1 Magic and +2 Charm. Gold Clover gives +1 Skill and +2 Luck. Toadstoolshed adds small amounts to many stats. Most full diets mix Cabbadillo and Dialaurel.
      • Levels. Pets level by picking up Exp crystals in battle. The Forbidden Ring, a reward of The Wisdom of Gaia (#3), splits the crystals between you, your NPC and your pet. The last feeding at level 99 sets the personality.
      • Selling. Jennifer in Domina buys pets; the price depends on species and level. The Sells figure below is the level 1 price.

      About Mana Compass

      What this is

      A fan-made companion for Legend of Mana Remastered: all 68 events in a missable-safe play order, skills, forging, farming and pets. Legend of Mana is a trademark of Square Enix. This guide is unofficial and not affiliated with or endorsed by Square Enix.

      Magic spells are not covered. Ability and technique names follow the Android remaster, with the original name shown where it changed. Quest, land and item names come from guides that mix both versions, so a few may not match your game.

      The play order is checked by a route solver, and forging numbers by a tempering simulator calibrated on in-game readings. Both are on the project on GitHub. All icons are simple original drawings, not artwork from the game.
